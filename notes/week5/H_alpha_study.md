# Ionization

The ionization by electronic impact is physically described by a continuation of the excitation process for continous states. When the main quantum number $q$ grows, the cross scetions of the excitations smoothly connects themselves to the ionization cross section. In the plasma, the effective ionization doesn't occour only for the fundamental state. The $S_{CR}$ coefficient gathers the direct ionization plus the ladder-like excitation-ionization, where the electrons are excited one after the other across the excited levels, until reaches the continumm

# Electronic excitation

Occurs by electron impact ($p + q \longrightarrow q + e$). The probability of the reaction is quantified by the cross-section $\sigma_{p,q}(E)$ or by the collisional force $\Omega_{p,q}(E)$. For transitions optically permited, the assyntotic cross-section are porportional to the absorption oscillator force $f_{p,q}$ For optically forbidden transitions ($f_{p,q} = 0$), the cross-section is smaller and get smaller quickly with the rise of electron energy. For positive ions, the coulombian atraction holds the cross-section finite in the energy celling; however, for neutron atoms, the cross-section grows from zero in the celling.

# Recombination

There are three main types of atomic mechanisms for the recombination:

## Radiative recombination

Capture of a free electron by an ion with the spontaneous emission of a photon ($z + e \longrightarrow (z-1)(p) + h\nu$).

## Three body recombination

Three particle colisional process ($z + e + e \longrightarrow (z-1)(p) + e), being the exact oposite of colisional ionization.

## Dielectronic recombination

Two stages process in ions, begins with the dielectronic capture of an incident electron that excites the electron from the atom, creating a double excited state, then the electron stabilizing radiative transition emits a satellite line. Its dominant in high temperature ions.

In the end, we have the collisional-radiative recombination coefficient, which is a effective total rate that counts the direct recombination to the ground state plus the radiative/colisional flux from all the excited states.

# Charge exchange

Occurs when an ion collides with a neutral atom and capture one or more eletrons from him.
